# Daily News Briefing — November 13, 2026

## 🤖 AI & LLMs

- Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen (Nov 12), a fast, low-cost model for agentic workflows that is rolling out worldwide in the Gemini app, Search, the Gemini API and AI Studio; Google says it finishes tasks 2.5x faster than its predecessor and makes things up more than 5x less often (Google's own figures). ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen))

## ⛏️ AI in Mining

- Envision Energy is selling remote Australian mines an off-grid power system that combines wind, solar, batteries and backup turbines, with its AI models forecasting weather and load and dispatching power in real time; it claims about 20% lower energy costs and over 80% renewable supply, but no mine has reported results yet. ([RenewEconomy](https://reneweconomy.com.au/envision-energys-new-ai-powered-system-can-help-mining-companies-decarbonise/))

## 🔎 AI in Mineral Exploration

- National Resource Limited reported copper-gold mineralization at eight porphyry discoveries in Pakistan's Chagai district from about 25,000 m of drilling, including 295 m at 0.43% Cu and 0.13 g/t Au, and is building a predictive AI exploration model with LUMS. The model isn't running yet and there is no formal resource estimate (Nov 13). ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2044284/copper-gold-deposits-identified-at-eight-sites-in-balochistans-chagai-district-mining-company))

## 🦾 Robotics Startups

- Munich-based SE3 raised a $30M Series A led by Accel (Nov 12) for "spatial intelligence" software that lets drones and other autonomous machines understand and navigate 3D space, about five months after a €5.5M seed round. ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/se3-30m-series-a-accel-spatial-intelligence-drones))

## 🔧 Hardware / Sensor Opportunities

- Melexis launched the MLX90901, its first tactile fingertip module, which senses 3D force (both normal pressure and shear) for humanoid robot hands, plus a DVK900901 evaluation kit for hand makers (Nov 12). *Opportunity: an off-the-shelf fingertip from a high-volume automotive chip supplier could make touch sensing a cheap, standard part rather than custom lab hardware; lucrative uses include retrofit kits that add slip detection to existing grippers, gentle picking of produce and e-commerce items, and touch-labeled datasets sold to robot-learning companies.* ([Robotics Tomorrow](https://www.roboticstomorrow.com/news/2026/11/12/melexis-gives-humanoid-robots-sense-of-touch-with-its-first-tactile-fingertip-module/27374))
