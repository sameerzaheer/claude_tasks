## 🔧 Hardware / Sensor Opportunities

- Microchip launched the ATFCD52 (Nov 10), a production chip that reads robot "skin": it converts up to 52-channel capacitive tactile arrays into data at below 1 fF resolution and up to 800 Hz, so a processor can estimate pressure and shear for grip control and slip detection; it comes with a 10×10-array evaluation kit. *Opportunity: a standard chip from a high-volume supplier lets small teams make cheap, large-area tactile skins instead of building custom electronics; lucrative uses include retrofit tactile pads for existing grippers in e-commerce and food picking, collision-sensing covers for cobots, and touch-labeled grasp datasets for robot-learning companies.* ([Microchip](https://www.microchip.com/en-us/about/news-releases/products/microchip-enhances-robotic-tactile-sensing-with-high-speed-capacitance-digitizer))
